The problem

Motor side effects are studied with tools that don’t travel.

Psychiatric medication can cause tremor, slowing and shrinking handwriting. Studies that measure these effects still rely on clinician rating scales or desktop software tied to one tablet in one lab.

Rated by eye

Coarse clinical scales depend on who scores them and miss small changes.

Locked to one lab

Desktop tools need installation and specific hardware at every site.

Devices differ

Sampling rate and screen size change between tablets, so raw numbers don’t compare.

Metrics are opaque

When the computation is hidden, results are hard to reproduce or publish.

How it works

From protocol to dataset in four steps.

The research team keeps ownership of the study, its ethics approval and its participants. Ductus Research is the measurement instrument.

  1. 01

    Set up the study

    Choose the task protocol, sites and number of sessions. Participants get pseudonymous codes.

    • Pick tasks from the library and set their order, hand and repetitions.
    • Add sites and a visit schedule, for example baseline, week 4 and week 12.
    • Generate participant codes. Names and contact details stay at the site, never on our servers.
    Research team
  2. 02

    Certify each tablet

    A two-minute check measures sampling rate and timing jitter, then calibrates millimetres with a ruler.

    • Site staff trace a test pattern while the app logs the real sampling rate and jitter.
    • A ruler held against the screen converts pixels to millimetres for that exact device.
    • A tablet that fails is blocked from the study until it passes again.
    Site staff
  3. 03

    Run sessions

    The tablet locks to the protocol. Every stylus sample is recorded, with a quality grade for the session.

    • The participant sees one task at a time, with short on-screen instructions.
    • Position, pressure, tilt and time are saved for every stylus sample.
    • The quality grade appears at the end, so a poor recording can be repeated on the spot.
    Participant
  4. 04

    Analyse and export

    Metrics are computed with a fixed, versioned definition and exported as CSV for statistical analysis.

    • Tremor, speed and rhythm metrics are computed shortly after each upload.
    • Every result records the version of the metric library that produced it.
    • One CSV per study, ready for R, SPSS or Python.
    Research team

Reproducible by design

Same participant, two tablets, matching numbers.

Results are only useful if another site can get them too. That is why device checks, open definitions and quality flags are built into every session.

Device certification

Each tablet passes a sampling and timing check and a ruler calibration before it can join a study.

Open, versioned metrics

Every measure is defined in an open-source library. Exports record the version used, so results can be cited.

Quality flags

A model trained on labelled sessions flags unusable recordings, such as an unfinished task or the hand resting on the screen.

metrics / tremor_frequencyv1.0
# Dominant tremor frequency in the spiral task
input:      spiral trace, resampled to 200 Hz
signal:     radial deviation from ideal path (mm)
filter:     band-pass 3–15 Hz, 4th-order Butterworth
spectrum:   Welch, 2 s windows, 50% overlap
output:     peak frequency (Hz) + peak power
valid_if:   capture_quality in (good, fair)
            and trace covers ≥ 3 turns

Flagship protocol In design

Motor side effects of psychiatric medication, designed together with clinical researchers. Sessions are repeated across the study so each participant is compared with their own baseline.

For research use

  • Not a medical device and not for diagnosis
  • No treatment recommendations or alerts
  • Used within studies approved by the research team
Technology

Built on Microsoft Azure.

Each study is isolated, data stays in an EU region and is encrypted at rest, and participants are identified only by code.
